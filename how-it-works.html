How an engagement runs

Six steps, the same for a two-truck shop or a twelve-location practice. The flag rules and the access are written down before the first report.

  1. 1

    The call

    Thirty minutes on your entities, your accounts, your systems and who does the books today. If the fit is not there we say so and tell you what we would do instead.

  2. 2

    Connect & baseline

    You connect the bank and card feeds read-only — or we take directed exports if you prefer — and we rebuild the last 12 to 24 months into the recurring register, the vendor list, the document checklist and your flag rules. One-time fee, about ten business days.

  3. 3

    The baseline report

    The first report is about the history, not the week: the creep that accumulated, the duplicates already paid, the documents that never arrived. Most clients recover the setup fee here or in the first quarter.

  4. 4

    The Tuesday cadence

    Monday we read the week. Tuesday morning the report arrives: cash trends, recurring changes, duplicate candidates, missing documents, anomalies explained. You act on what matters and ignore the rest.

  5. 5

    Flags and escalations

    Anything large or strange is escalated the same day, with its explanation. You set the thresholds; we respect them and propose better ones as we learn your patterns.

  6. 6

    Month end and beyond

    Your bookkeeper closes a categorized, explained ledger in less time. Each quarter we re-baseline, tune the rules against what you actually acted on, and review what the monitoring caught.

How flags earn trust

A monitoring service is only as good as the worst flag it sends at 7 a.m. These are the rules every one of them follows.

Every flag explained

A flag with no explanation is work you gave yourself. Each one carries what it is, why it surfaced against the trailing pattern, and where to look first.

Evidence attached

The transaction, the prior occurrences, the vendor history, the document that is missing — attached to the flag, not filed in another system.

You set the rules

Thresholds, sensitive categories, vendors to ignore, who receives what. The rules are written down at setup and changed only on your word.

Read-only, always

We can see the money; we can never move it. Bank connections are view-only and revocable in one click. Watching and moving are different jobs, and we keep them apart by design.

What a flag looks like on paper

What
The transaction, vendor or document, identified precisely — invoice number, amount, date, account.
Why
The rule it broke, with the trailing pattern it broke against: the range, the cadence, the history.
First check
One sentence: where a person looks first to confirm or dismiss it.
Urgency
Escalated the same day above your thresholds; otherwise it waits for Tuesday, ranked with the rest.

Access and data

You should be able to revoke everything we hold in one morning. That is the test each design decision has to pass.

Your accounts

Bank and card connections live in your name, read-only. Revoke access any time; nothing else breaks.

Your documents

Documents stay in your drive or document system. We read what you point us at and list what is missing; we do not keep a second copy of your file.

Your ledger

Categorized exports land in your accounting file — QuickBooks, Xero or CSV. Your bookkeeper keeps the ledger of record.

Your history

Leave and you take the recurring register, the vendor list, the flag rules and every report ever delivered.

The details — encryption, sub-processors, retention — are on the trust page.

What we need from you

  • Thirty minutes for the first call, and a decision-maker on it
  • Bank and card feeds connected read-only, or directed exports if you prefer
  • Your chart of accounts, or permission to propose one
  • Point us at where the documents live — the drive, the document system, the shoebox
  • An email address that reaches a person on Tuesday mornings
