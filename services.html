Services

One weekly report, six disciplines behind it. The first four watch the money; the last two make what they find readable enough to act on. Each has a published price and a place in the plans.

Weekly cash reports

Month-end statements tell you what already happened. By the time the books close, the duplicate payment cleared and the price increase renewed. The cash section of the weekly report shows position, inflows, outflows and the trend against the trailing four weeks, with a runway comment written for your business, not a template.

  • Cash position at week close
  • Net cash against the trailing four-week average
  • Runway comment
  • Collections watch

Recurring expense watch

Recurring costs grow a few percent at a time, in the middle of statements nobody reads line by line. At setup we build the recurring register from up to 24 months of history; every week after that we diff it — new charges, re-priced charges, charges that stopped — and bring you the changes while they are still cheap to fix.

  • The recurring register
  • Weekly diff
  • Renewal calendar
  • Overlap and unused detection

Duplicate invoice detection

Double payments hide in fast-moving accounts payable: the same amount on different dates, the same invoice number with a typo, the ACH that followed the card by a week. Every week we compare what was paid against what was invoiced, across accounts, and bring back the candidates — with the evidence attached.

  • Fuzzy matching across vendor
  • Same-week and cross-month checks
  • Card-versus-bank cross-posting
  • Subscription double-billing after a price change or a card expiry

Missing document alerts

Gaps in the file become surprises: at tax time, at audit, at renewal, in the due-diligence room. We keep the checklist of what should exist for every vendor, account and month, watch for what does not arrive, and report what is missing while there is still time to get it.

  • Document checklist per vendor
  • Statement arrival tracking for bank
  • PO-versus-invoice matching gaps
  • Expiry watch on insurance certificates

Anomaly explanations

Most tools raise flags; somebody still opens five tabs to figure out whether each one matters. Every anomaly we surface comes with its explanation: what it is, why it looks unusual against the trailing pattern, and where to look first. The AI reads everything; a person writes the report.

  • New payees and first-time charges
  • Amounts outside their trailing ranges
  • Timing breaks
  • Descriptor translation

Categorization & summaries

Books are only as useful as they are readable. Every transaction is categorized to your chart of accounts, vendor names are normalized — the six spellings of the same supplier become one — and the week's changes are summarized: what moved, what repeated, what is new.

  • Every transaction categorized to your chart of accounts
  • Vendor normalization and roll-ups
  • The weekly change summary
  • Month-to-date and quarter-to-date running views
